The moment that defined a generation of readers and critics alike. The story goes deeper than anyone had anticipated when the first reports came through on a Tuesday afternoon in November. It was a cold morning — the kind of morning where you remember exactly where you were. The press had gathered outside the building for hours, waiting. Nothing about the day felt ordinary.

The editor said nothing for a long time. He looked at the photograph on the desk. Then he said: print it. Those two words would become the most discussed editorial decision of the decade. Some said it was brave. Others said it was reckless. Most agreed it was necessary.

What followed was weeks of coverage, counter-coverage, and silence. Sources refused to speak on record. Others spoke only in general terms, careful not to implicate themselves or the organisations they represented. The truth, as it usually does, arrived slowly and in pieces.

There is a particular kind of exhaustion that comes from waiting for a story to resolve itself. Journalists know it well. You follow every lead, return every call, sit in draughty corridors outside rooms where decisions are being made. And then one day it simply ends — not with a revelation but with a statement, carefully worded, released on a Friday afternoon.
